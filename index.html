01

VIZIT / SCIENTIFIC DIRECTION

Changing what “better” means.

Problem framing · Representation learning · Evaluation

An image that attracts attention is not necessarily an image that helps someone choose a product. I brought that distinction into the learning problem.

The context

Brands use Vizit to evaluate the imagery on their ecommerce pages. Engagement-based learning offered a useful signal, but the product needed guidance relevant to shopping decisions.

The decision I owned

I led a shift toward commerce-related learning objectives. That meant revisiting the target, learning from imperfect labels, and evaluating the resulting signal against the problem the product was meant to solve.

The leadership work

I connected the research agenda to customer needs and made the scientific trade-offs explicit. Progress meant improving the relevance of the learning problem, alongside the model itself.

What changed

A new learning direction and scoring approach centered on commerce relevance. Predictive evidence supports that direction; proving that an image change causes more purchases is a separate evaluation.

02

VIZIT / PRODUCT JUDGMENT

A score customers can reason with.

Interpretability · KPI definition · Cross-functional delivery

A competitive ranking can tell you where you stand without telling you what to improve. I made the case for changing the meaning of the score.

The context

The overall product-page score expressed competitive rank. Customers also needed an understandable metric they could use to guide improvements and track a KPI.

The decision I owned

I replaced the learned model that combined our signals into an overall score with a more interpretable formula. Machine learning continued to power the underlying signals. The aggregation and explanation changed.

The leadership work

I advocated for interpretability as a product requirement and accepted the trade-off: the new number could not retain the old percentile’s meaning. I evaluated the formula against a conversion-related outcome and partnered with product and engineering to deliver it.

What changed

The formula reached production through a controlled release with a clearer scoring definition. Delivery and evaluation are established; customer adoption and causal sales lift are separate measures.

03

VIZIT / CUSTOMER-FACING SCIENCE

Make the evidence useful. Then make it scale.

Causal inference · Customer communication · Analytics automation

Enterprise customers needed more than a model score. They needed an understandable account of performance and a repeatable way to examine it.

The context

Measuring commerce imagery requires separating an observed change from the effect attributable to the intervention. Delivering that analysis repeatedly also creates a substantial operational workload.

The work I led

I applied difference-in-differences and experiment design to customer impact studies, then built self-serve dashboards to make the analysis repeatable.

The leadership work

I presented and defended the methodology directly with customer stakeholders. That brought scientific reasoning, its assumptions, and the customer's business question into the same conversation.

Reported career results

60 impact studies delivered and more than 5,000 analyst hours automated. These are delivery and efficiency outcomes; any causal interpretation of a study depends on its design and assumptions.

Team building · Customer intelligence · Executive communication
