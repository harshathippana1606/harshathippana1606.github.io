How I approach the work

Open a method to explore the thinking.

01RCTs / A/B testsRandomized experiments

Start with the decision, then design the test around it.

  • Choose the unit of assignment and randomize into comparable treatment and control groups.
  • Define the primary metric, guardrails, and a practically meaningful effect before the test.
  • Plan sample size and duration for adequate power; check execution and uncertainty before drawing conclusions.
02Difference-in-differencesQuasi-experiments

When randomization is unavailable, make the assumptions visible.

  • Find a credible comparison group and examine differences before the intervention.
  • Assess the plausibility of parallel trends; pre-period checks inform that judgment but cannot prove it.
  • Investigate concurrent changes, spillovers, and sensitivity to the analysis choices before attributing an effect.
03Communication / repeatabilityDecision & scale

A study is useful when people understand what they can do with it.

  • Explain the estimated effect, its uncertainty, and its practical size in the customer’s context.
  • Keep conclusions within the design’s limits and distinguish evidence from assumptions.
  • Turn repeatable analysis into dashboards, so the next decision does not require starting over.

03

VIZIT / CAUSAL INFERENCE & EXPERIMENTATION

From observed change to defensible impact.

Causal inference · Difference-in-differences · Experiment design

Enterprise customers needed to understand whether imagery changes made a difference. I scaled causal-inference impact studies from experimental design to direct customer presentations.

The context

Measuring commerce imagery requires separating an observed change from the effect attributable to the intervention. Delivering that analysis repeatedly also creates a substantial operational workload.

The work I led

I applied difference-in-differences and experiment design to customer impact studies. I carried the work through analysis and customer presentations, then built self-serve dashboards to make repeated analysis scalable.

The leadership work

I presented and defended the methodology directly with customer stakeholders. That brought scientific reasoning, its assumptions, and the customer's business question into the same conversation.

Reported career results

60 impact studies delivered and more than 5,000 analyst hours automated. These are delivery and efficiency outcomes; any causal interpretation of a study depends on its design and assumptions.
